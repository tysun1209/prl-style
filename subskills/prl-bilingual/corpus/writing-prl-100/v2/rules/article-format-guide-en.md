# PRL-like whole-article guide (English working manuscript)

This guide is the whole-article companion to the 14 English v2 rule cards. It
is a conditional writing guide distilled from the 80-paper development split,
not an APS production specification. The 20-paper holdout is reserved for
validation and does not choose these instructions.

## Build the argument before polishing sentences

Write a one-line claim ledger before drafting: physical question, established
result, decisive evidence, conditions, comparison, uncertainty, and remaining
boundary. Every paragraph and figure should have one job in that ledger. Keep
the claim at the level supplied by the facts: a measured trend, a derived
statement under assumptions, a numerical comparison, and a mechanistic
interpretation are different evidence classes.

## Title and abstract

The title names the object or phenomenon and the discriminating result. Keep a
regime, geometry, or control qualifier when removing it would make the title
broader than the evidence. Avoid novelty adjectives and method-only titles.

The abstract is a compact map of the Letter. Make the question or obstacle,
approach, principal result, and bounded implication recoverable. The corpus
contains direct-result, method-first, measurement-capability, and correction-of-
an-assumption openings, so choose the order that follows the dependency of the
science. Include a number only when its conditions and comparison are known.
Do not force a four-sentence template, cite a literature survey, or leave the
main result implicit.

## Opening and model

The first body paragraphs move from a concrete physical or methodological
obstacle to the question this Letter can answer. State the relevant comparison
or scope near that question. Then introduce the model, apparatus, data source,
or algorithm with the assumptions needed by later claims: geometry, regime,
boundary condition, control, sampling, finite size, approximation, and
uncertainty convention.

An explicit section heading is optional. A flowing Letter may use no headings,
inline labels, or a small number of headings such as Model or Conclusions. Do
not add conventional Introduction/Methods/Results headings merely to imitate a
template. When equations carry the method, name their physical role in prose
before and after the display; extracted PDF equations require source checking.

## Results and figure sequence

Organize results as an evidence sequence rather than a list of figure numbers.
For each step, state the quantity or pattern, give the relevant panel or
calculation, name the baseline or comparison, quantify the result at the
reported scope, and then interpret it. Establish a structural or calibration
fact before using it to interpret dynamics when the dependency requires that
order. Keep multiple diagnostics distinct when they test different mechanisms.

Give every figure or panel a distinct evidentiary job. A caption tells the
reader what is plotted or shown, how visual encodings map to quantities, what
conditions apply, and what comparison matters. A schematic is not a measured
confirmation. The body should explain why the figure enters the argument and
should not repeat every plotted element.

Quantitative language stays local: a pooled mean is not a condition-resolved
claim; a finite-range fit is not a universal law; agreement with a reference is
not automatically a mechanism test. Use measured, calculated, derived, found,
consistent with, or suggests according to the evidence. Reserve demonstrates,
universal, robust, and significant for cases whose definitions and support are
actually present.

## Discussion and closing

Separate the observed or derived result from the mechanism inferred from it.
After answering the opening question, state what the evidence does not decide:
an alternative mechanism, an untested regime, a finite-size limit, or a needed
control. The last substantive paragraph should return to the demonstrated
result and then give a concrete boundary, application, or next test. A future
extension is not evidence already obtained. The close may be unheaded or
labelled Conclusion, Discussion, or Outlook.

## Format and production boundary

This guide controls argument and working-manuscript organization. It does not
claim publisher production typography, page geometry, abstract limits, or
acceptance. Use the local REVTeX template for compilable drafts, the separate
typography reference for Times New Roman/SimSun defaults, and the layout
checker plus rendered-page inspection for delivery. The figure policy remains
inward ticks and no unsolicited small explanatory notes on the canvas.

## Evidence and limitations

The derivation ledger records 80 development reviews, 20 holdout reviews, 53
development heading records labelled unheaded or mostly unheaded, 78 papers
with substantive figure sequences, 216 figure-sequence entries, and a source-
located closing move for every development paper. These are review-label
counts, not journal-wide frequencies. All active cards retain source hashes,
review IDs, and reviewed block IDs. The reviews were agent full-maintext
reviews, not independent human editorial judgments; equations, glyph fidelity,
and scientific correctness still require author verification.
